# Human cost-factor field calibration

The original v1 cost-factor recordings are numerically reproducible, but their
resource/rating names were reversed. The adapter called mode0 and wrote the
GROWTH field while naming those inputs minerals/INDUSTRY. It called mode1 and
wrote INDUSTRY while naming those inputs nutrients/GROWTH. The two rule-global
names were correspondingly reversed.

The new `game.faction_social_ratings.v1` observations establish these meanings
through the actual original Social Engineering screen after initialized first
base setup. Contrasting Consciousness and Drone ratings distinguish the fields;
controlled 17/23 and 23/17 source-rule values distinguish the adjacent globals.
Every rating observation requires independent rendered labels/numerals to agree
with guarded native data. The opening dialog alone is too early: ratings can
still be zero before initialization completes.

| Actual quantity | Physical data field | Original cost-factor mode |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| GROWTH / nutrients | 0x0096CC44 + faction*0x20CC; rule 0x00949750 | 0 |
| INDUSTRY / minerals | 0x0096CC50 + faction*0x20CC; rule 0x00949754 | 1 |

All four old input/evidence groups remain unchanged and are hash-guarded in
`test_factors_v2.py`. Their explicit provenance registry is
`factor-v1-historical-catalog.json`. They are excluded from the default passing
totals. Historical adapter replay preserves the raw numeric experiments and
does not validate the old semantic labels.

The v2 inputs reuse the physical numeric coverage under correct field names.
New answers execute the original instructions again in independent repeated
runs; no historical answer is renamed, edited or generated by GLSMAC. Twenty
additional zero-rating witnesses support installed-rule browser tests.

The production helpers now use the correctly named kernels. Previously shipped
gameplay queried rating0 for both quantities, where the kernels agree. The new
source integration additionally applies installed initial GROWTH/INDUSTRY.
Other social bonuses, policy changes, per-base modifiers, population booms and
computer-player cost rules remain outside these contracts.
